If you're planning to study in Germany, the blocked account — called a *Sperrkonto* — is one of the most important steps in your student visa journey. Yet it's also one of the most confusing for Indian students and their parents. This guide explains exactly what it is, how much you need in 2026, and how to open one, in simple terms.

## What is a blocked account (Sperrkonto)?

A blocked account is a special German bank account that proves you can financially support yourself during your first year of studies. The German embassy requires it as part of your student visa application. You deposit a fixed amount before you travel, and the money is "blocked" — meaning you can only withdraw a set amount each month once you arrive in Germany.

Think of it as a guarantee: it shows the German government you won't run out of money as a student.

## How much money do you need in 2026?

For 2026, you need to deposit **€11,904 for the year**. This works out to **€992 per month** across twelve months. Once you arrive in Germany, you can withdraw up to €992 each month to cover your living expenses — rent, food, transport, and other daily costs.

This amount is set by the German government and is reviewed periodically, so it has increased over the years. Always confirm the current figure before you apply, as it can change. As of 2026, €11,904 is the required amount.

## Step-by-step: how to open your blocked account

The process is straightforward when you know the steps:

First, choose your provider (Fintiba or Expatrio) and create an account online. Next, complete the application with your passport and personal details. You'll then receive your blocked account details and transfer the required €11,904 from India — usually via an international bank transfer or a supported payment method. Once the funds arrive and are confirmed, you receive a blocking confirmation certificate. This certificate is what you submit with your student visa application.

After you arrive in Germany and activate your account, the monthly release of €992 begins, giving you steady access to your funds throughout the year.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Many students face visa delays because of small errors with the blocked account. The most common are: depositing the wrong (outdated) amount, starting the process too late, choosing an insurance package that doesn't meet visa requirements, or making transfer errors that delay fund confirmation. Because this account is tied directly to your visa, even a small mistake can push back your entire timeline.
